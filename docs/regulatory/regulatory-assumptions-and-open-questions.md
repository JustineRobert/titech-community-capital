# Regulatory Assumptions and Open Questions

## Assumptions for engineering

- TITech does not hold member funds in the core model.
- TITech uses licensed/regulated partners for regulated payment execution and custody.
- The institution/customer remains accountable for its own member relationship and financial obligations.
- TITech's ledger is a control and records layer, not an assertion that TITech is the legal holder of member assets.
- Lending decisions remain with the appropriately authorized lender.
- Data-protection roles are contract-specific and must not be hard-coded as universal labels.

## Open legal questions

1. Does any proposed TITech payment orchestration flow meet the statutory definition of a payment service or payment system operator in the intended configuration?
2. What contract language is required to maintain the no-custody model?
3. When does TITech's reconciliation/control role create a licensing or oversight consequence?
4. What activities, if any, require sandbox participation before commercial launch?
5. Which institution/partner is the legal AML/KYC accountable party for each flow?
6. Which entity is controller and which is processor for each data flow?
7. What cross-border transfer conditions apply to East African expansion?
8. What consumer-protection obligations flow through partner agreements?
9. What record-retention periods apply to payment, ledger, support and consent evidence?
10. Which product expansions would move TITech into a new regulatory category?
11. Does payroll/disbursement orchestration alter the payment-services analysis?
12. What licensing perimeter applies if TITech later introduces direct credit products?
