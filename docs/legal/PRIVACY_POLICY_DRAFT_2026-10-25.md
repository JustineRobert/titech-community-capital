# TITech Community Capital — Privacy Policy

**Intended operator identity:** TITech Capital LTD  
**Product:** TITech Community Capital  
**Version:** `draft-2026-10-25`  
**Publication date:** To be set after review.

This notice must be mapped to the live service, actual data flows, controller/processor contracts, subprocessor register and approved retention schedule before publication. It deliberately does not claim that every data category or feature below is currently active.

## 1. Who is responsible

The intended operator identity is TITech Capital LTD, subject to verification of its registration details and its legal role for each processing purpose. Depending on the service and contract, TITech may act as controller, joint controller, processor or service provider. A SACCO, cooperative, savings group, NGO, insurer, employer, payment provider or other institution may independently determine purposes and means and have its own obligations. The deployed notice must identify the right controller and contact for the relevant service rather than assume every institution shares one role.

## 2. Categories of information

Depending on the enabled features, information may include account identifiers and contact details; authentication and session metadata; institution, group and member records; permissions and consent history; support messages; device, network and security diagnostics; payment instructions and provider attempts; financial transaction, ledger, receipt and reconciliation records; and audit/operational events.

If a responsible institution lawfully activates KYC, assisted refugee/asylum-seeker onboarding, insurance, claims, fundraising, education support or similar workflows, additional documents, eligibility data, beneficiary information, policy/claim references or sensitive records may be involved. Collect only what is needed for an identified purpose. Do not enable a feature merely because source code is present; assess legal basis, necessity, access, safeguards and retention first.

The product owner must verify all fields, telemetry and technologies actually used—including cookies, analytics, location, biometrics, children’s data, marketing pixels and browser storage—before this policy is final. The presence of a category in this draft does not prove that it is collected.

## 3. Purposes and lawful bases

Potential purposes include account creation and security; authentication and access control; institution/group administration; maintenance of records; authorised payment-instruction orchestration; reconciliation; prevention of fraud and abuse; support; audit evidence; legal compliance; and user-requested optional services. For each activity, the relevant controller must document the legal basis and give required information. Consent must not be assumed to be the basis for every necessary operation, and no contractual or legitimate-interest basis should be assumed to justify an unrelated use. Optional marketing and secondary uses must be handled separately where required.

## 4. Consent and choices

Where consent is used, it must be free, informed, specific, unambiguous and purpose-bound and must be recorded. Withdrawal must be supported where required; it does not invalidate processing lawfully completed before withdrawal, and it does not erase a separate legal obligation to retain records. Agreement to the Terms of Service and acknowledgement of this notice are not blanket consent to every processing purpose. Distinct consent—such as optional marketing or sharing with a capital provider—must be separately presented and recorded where required.

## 5. Sharing and recipients

Depending on the service, information may be shared with the institution administering the account/group; authorised staff and support personnel; contracted hosting, security and communications providers; a payment provider to carry out an authorised instruction; and courts, regulators or other recipients where required or permitted by law. Optional sharing with insurers, capital providers, donors, risk partners or other third parties requires a documented purpose, authority and consent when applicable. A tenant-scoped, purpose-bound data-sharing request must not be processed without the required active consent and approval.

Maintain a current list of subprocessors and recipients, the data each can access, their role, hosting locations, contract/safeguards, and an owner for reviewing changes. The final notice should identify categories and meaningful recipient information as required by law.

## 6. Cross-border processing

Before publication, inventory actual hosting regions, subprocessors, support access and all cross-border transfers. Check applicable Ugandan transfer restrictions and other jurisdictional rules, implement required contractual/technical safeguards, and give the required notice. This draft does not claim a particular transfer mechanism or adequacy finding has been completed.

## 7. Retention, deletion and legal holds

Retain information only as long as necessary for the identified purpose and under the approved record-class schedule. Statutory recordkeeping, payment reconciliation, audit, security investigations, disputes, financial obligations and legal holds may require certain records to be retained. Once retention is no longer justified, securely delete or anonymise information under a documented process. Financial ledgers and audit histories should not be destructively edited to implement a deletion request; evaluate lawful minimisation, restriction or linkage removal alternatives instead.

## 8. Security

Controls should include least privilege, server-enforced tenant isolation and RBAC, secure authentication and token handling, encryption in transit and appropriate at-rest protection, controlled key management, audit monitoring, dependency/security updates, backups, tested restoration and incident response. Sensitive identity files must only pass through the approved private-document path. Malware scanning, private storage permissions, access logging, backups and key-recovery must be verified in the actual deployed environment. No system can guarantee absolute security.

## 9. Financial and institution-specific data

Financial information should be available only to authorised roles and within the correct tenant/institution scope. The platform should distinguish an instruction, provider attempt, verified response, settlement evidence, ledger posting and reconciliation outcome. A timeout, pending state or unverified callback must not be shown as final settlement. Lending, insurance, payment, fundraising, risk or credit-related services may require additional notices and disclosures before activation.

## 10. Rights and data-subject requests

Subject to applicable law and the facts of the processing, individuals may be able to request access to information about processing, access or correction of personal data, deletion or restriction where available, object to particular processing, withdraw consent where it is the basis, or complain to the competent authority. Statutory exceptions, lawful retention and identity verification may apply. The production notice must specify how to submit a request, how identity is verified safely, which controller responds, and how deadlines are measured.

## 11. Children and assisted onboarding

Children’s data, refugee/asylum-seeker evidence, health/disability information, insurance/claims material and other sensitive records require extra care. Before activating these workflows, review necessity, lawful basis, representative/guardian safeguards, fair treatment, data minimisation, access, retention, staff procedures and partner obligations. Assisted or offline onboarding is not an exception to privacy and security. Offline data must be protected and must never be treated as authoritative financial confirmation until validated by the server.

## 12. Cookies, local storage and telemetry

Audit the deployed web/mobile clients for essential and non-essential cookies; access/refresh-token storage; browser local/session storage; IndexedDB; analytics; crash reporting; metrics; pixels; and third-party scripts. The final notice must name actual technologies, purposes, providers, lifetimes and controls where applicable. Access tokens must remain in memory if that is the established architecture; refresh tokens should use the approved HttpOnly cookie strategy. Diagnostics must not expose tokens, passwords, session IDs, payment details, identity documents or unnecessary identifiers.

## 13. Changes

Track policy changes by version, publication date, approval record and summary. Notify users of material changes; obtain new consent where required. Preserve historical notices and acceptance evidence under the approved governance and retention rules.

## 14. Regulatory and contact verification

Review this notice against the current Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021, any applicable amendments and official guidance, plus any sector-specific rules and contractual obligations. Confirm whether TITech and the relevant institutional partners must register with the competent authority, designate a data-protection officer or perform further assessments. These obligations depend on the actual role, operation and facts and are not deemed complete by this draft.

Before publication, enter company-approved privacy and registered-office details, define response ownership and escalation, verify the complaint/regulator contact, and have qualified Uganda privacy counsel approve the final policy.
